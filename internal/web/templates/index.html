How the score works

We rank every market on the same seven things. Each one becomes a percentile: a country at 80 on savings scores better than 80% of the markets with savings data. The score is the weighted average of those percentiles. If a country is missing a factor, we drop it and reweight the rest, so no one is marked down for data we do not have.

ConditionWeightWhat it measures
EV/EBIT0.30Median enterprise value to EBIT. Ignores debt and tax, so it compares better across countries.
P/B0.20Median price to book. What you pay for the assets on the balance sheet.
Savings0.15Gross national savings as a share of GDP. Domestic capital for investment.
Growth0.10Five-year average GDP growth. Backward-looking, so it counts for less than savings.
Trade0.10External balance on goods and services as a share of GDP. Whether the country earns more abroad than it spends.
Debt0.10General government gross debt as a share of GDP, latest completed year. Government only, not private or external debt.
Free market0.05Fraser's Economic Freedom of the World rating and its five-year change. A soft signal, not a veto.

Valuation is half the score. Growth is a trailing average, so it counts for less than savings. The freedom rating is a nudge, not a gate: a closed market can still be cheap enough to be interesting.

This is a shortlist, not a buy list. A high rank means a market looks cheap next to the others, with conditions that look supportive. It does not tell you when the price will turn.
